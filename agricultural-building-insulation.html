What an agricultural building insulation contractor does

An agricultural building insulation contractor insulates and seals the building itself: roof, walls, doors and the rooms inside. The systems that run a barn, from fans to heaters to waterers, belong to ag equipment dealers and other trades.

The short answer

How to insulate an agricultural building

First decide if the building is cold or warm. A cold barn or machine shed usually needs a drip barrier on the roof, not full insulation. A heated shop, a warm livestock barn or produce storage needs insulated walls and roof with a sealed vapor layer. In every case, cover the insulation where animals, birds and rodents can reach it, and plan ventilation with the people who design it.

Cold barn or warm barn

Drip barrier or full insulation?

A cold barn runs close to outside temperature and relies on ventilation. Ontario's agriculture ministry advises covering the roof steel of a cold horse barn with at least R-4 of insulation as a drip barrier. A warm building is a different job. Switch between them.

Cold barn or shed

Inside stays close to outside temperature, and ventilation does the work. The goal is just to keep the roof steel from dripping.

  • Drip barrier under the roof steel, at least R-4
  • Walls usually left open or lightly covered
  • Ventilation kept open and working
  • Insulation covered where animals reach

Source: OMAFRA factsheet, Horse Barn Ventilation.

Roof drips

Stopping a pole barn roof from raining inside

South Dakota State University Extension puts it simply: "Moisture issues in barns are the result of condensation." Warm, moist air hits a cold steel roof and drips back down on equipment, hay and animals. The fix depends on whether the barn is already built.

  • Existing or new

    Spray foam under the roof

    Closed-cell foam sprayed on the underside of the steel. Seals and insulates in one step, with no gaps for moist air. Covered where animals or people can reach it.

  • Existing or new

    Liner with insulation

    Insulation held up by a continuous liner under the purlins or trusses. A clean, bright ceiling that keeps birds and dust off the insulation.

  • New build

    Blanket under the roof steel

    Faced blanket rolled out over the purlins before the roof steel goes on. Easy during construction, hard to add later.

  • New build, building supplier

    Factory drip-stop

    A felt-like layer bonded to the roof panels by the manufacturer. Ordered with the steel from your building supplier, not something we add on site.

Quote from SDSU Extension, Sheep Facilities and Moisture.

Rodents, birds and livestock

Keeping mice, birds and animals out of the insulation

Exposed insulation in a farm building gets nested in, pecked at and chewed. The answer is not a magic material. It is covering the insulation and closing the ways in.

Way in: the wall base

Mice and rats

They tunnel into loose blankets and soft foam at the wall base. We seal gaps at the bottom of the wall and cover low insulation with a tough liner or kick board.

Way in: open purlins

Birds

They nest on open purlins and pull at facings. A liner under the purlins or covered foam takes away the ledges and the nesting material.

Way in: anything in reach

Livestock and horses

Animals rub, kick and chew anything in reach. Wherever they can touch the wall, the insulation sits behind a liner, kick board or panel strong enough to take it.

Livestock barns

Moisture, ammonia and washdown in livestock barns

Animals breathe out moisture all day, manure gives off ammonia, and pens get washed down. That combination is hard on insulation facings and fasteners, and it is why ventilation and envelope have to be planned together.

Moisture

Insulation sealed against the roof and walls keeps warm, wet air off cold steel. Ventilation removes the moisture itself.

Ammonia

Ammonia attacks bare steel and thin facings over time. We choose facings, liners and fasteners that hold up, and seal the insulation behind them.

Planned togetherEnvelope and ventilation

Washdown

Where pens are cleaned with water, finishes have to be washable and insulation fully covered, so it never takes on water.

Ventilation

Fans, inlets and controls are designed and sold by your ag equipment dealer. We build the envelope to match their plan.

Hay and fire

Hay storage: dry roof first, insulation second

Most hay barns do not need full insulation. They need a roof that does not drip and good airflow. And the biggest fire risk in a hay barn is the hay itself: extension safety guidance says moisture content is the main factor that causes hay to spontaneously combust, with bales above 20 percent moisture at risk.

Heated farm shop

Turning a cold building into a shop you can work in

A farm shop is where repairs happen in January. Heating a bare metal building is like heating the outdoors. Full insulation, sealed walls and a warm office corner make it usable all winter.

Codes and permits

Agricultural buildings and the building code

Building codes based on the International Building Code list "Agricultural buildings" and "Livestock shelters" in Group U, utility and miscellaneous. How your county applies that, and what permits it wants, varies. Changing how a barn is used can change the rules.

  • Stays agricultural

    Barns, sheds, storage

    Often treated as Group U. Your county decides what permits apply. We follow what they require.

  • Ask first

    Shop with an office

    Adding heated, occupied space inside a barn can bring other code requirements. Check with your county before the build.

  • Different rules

    Events or living space

    A barn used for events or as living quarters is usually treated very differently. That is a conversation with your county and designer first.

Questions

Agricultural building insulation contractor FAQs

Prefer to just ask?Call and talk to the crew that will actually be on your roof deck. (303) 854-6221
01What does an agricultural building insulation contractor do?

An agricultural building insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals farm and ranch buildings: roof drip barriers, post-frame and metal wall insulation, liners that protect insulation from animals and pests, and farm shop offices. Barn fans, heaters, refrigeration and grain systems are installed by other trades.

02How do I stop my pole barn roof from dripping?

Keep warm, moist air off the cold roof steel. In an existing barn that usually means closed-cell spray foam under the roof or a liner with insulation. In a new barn it can also be a blanket under the roof steel or factory drip-stop on the panels, ordered from your building supplier.

03Does every barn need to be insulated?

No. A cold machine shed or cold barn usually only needs a drip barrier under the roof. Ontario's agriculture ministry suggests at least R-4 on the roof steel of a cold horse barn as a drip barrier. Heated shops, warm livestock barns and produce storage need full insulation.

04Will mice get into the insulation?

Rodents tunnel into loose or exposed insulation, especially at the wall base. We seal the bottom of the wall and cover low insulation with a tough liner or kick board so they cannot reach it.

05Can animals chew spray foam?

Animals rub, kick and chew anything they can reach. Wherever livestock or horses can touch a wall, the insulation should sit behind a liner, kick board or panel strong enough to take it.

06Should I insulate my hay barn?

Usually not fully. Hay barns need a roof that does not drip and good airflow. The bigger fire risk is hay baled too wet: extension safety guidance says bales above 20 percent moisture can heat up and spontaneously combust.

07How do you insulate post-frame walls?

Post-frame walls have posts set far apart with horizontal girts, so there is no standard stud cavity. We use spray foam against the steel or insulation between girts and an added frame, a sealed vapor layer in heated buildings, and an interior liner to protect it.

08Do I need a permit to insulate a barn?

It depends on your county. Codes based on the IBC list agricultural buildings and livestock shelters in Group U, and counties treat them in different ways. Turning a barn into a shop with an office, an event space or living quarters can bring very different rules.

09How much does agricultural building insulation cost?

Agricultural building insulation cost depends most on whether the building needs a drip barrier or full insulation, then on size and height, liners and covers, removal of old insulation, clearing the building and travel to the site. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
